# Outreach guide

Informational conversations ("could I ask you a few questions about your job?") are how people who don't know what they want find out fast, and how most first jobs actually get found. The messages that work are short, specific, human, and easy to say yes to.

## Message rules

- **Her words.** Build from what she says out loud. Trim; don't polish. If she wouldn't say a phrase to a person, cut it.
- **Short.** Email: about 100 words or fewer. LinkedIn connection note: 200 characters or fewer (the limit for many free accounts; count them and show the count).
- **One true, specific line about them.** Their path, their work, a shared school. Something they'd recognise. No flattery ("I'm so impressed by your amazing career").
- **One easy ask.** "Would you be up for a 15-minute call sometime in the next few weeks?", or a single question they could answer by reply. Never ask for a job or a referral in a first message.
- **One line about her.** "I just finished an M.Sc. in chemistry and I'm working out where to go next."
- **No AI tells.** No bullet points or bold in messages. Avoid "I hope this finds you well", "I'd love to pick your brain", "passionate about", "leverage", "synergy", "impactful", "reach out" twice, lists of three adjectives, and a closing aphorism. Read it aloud: if it sounds like a cover letter, cut it.
- **Easy out.** "No worries at all if you're busy." People say yes more often when no is easy.

## Template shapes (fill with her words)

Email:

```
Subject: <specific: "Chemistry M.Sc. with a question about application science">

Hi <first name>,

<the specific line about them>. <one line about her>. <why them, in a few words>.

Would you be up for a 15-minute call in the next couple of weeks? I'd love to hear what your first year looked like. No worries at all if you're busy.

Thanks,
<her name>
```

LinkedIn note (≤200 characters):

```
Hi <first name>, I just finished a chem M.Sc. at <school> and I'm curious how you got into <their field>. Would you be open to a quick chat sometime? Totally fine if not!
```

## Follow-up (once, after a week)

```
Hi <first name>, just floating this back up in case it got buried. No worries at all if now's not a good time.
```

## Thank-you (after a chat, same day or next)

Three lines: thanks, one specific thing they said that helped, and what she's going to do with it. If they offered an intro, a gentle reminder in the same note.

## Before a conversation

If she asks for help preparing: five questions at most, the most important first. Good defaults:

1. "How did you end up in this role?"
2. "What does a normal week look like?"
3. "What surprised you in your first year?"
4. "What do people who do well here have in common?"
5. "Is there anyone else you think I should talk to?"

Add one question from the open questions on her map (`type: chat`) that this person can answer.

## Tracking

Status moves: `to-draft` → `drafted` (guide) → `sent` (only when she says so) → `replied` → `chatted` → `thanked`, or `parked` at any point. One follow-up, ever. 5–10 new messages a week is a healthy pace.
