# Question bank

For about-me sessions and for the "why" after strong reactions. One question at a time. Reflect what you heard in fewer words before the next.

## Universal moves

- **Concrete beats abstract.** When she says "something meaningful" or "something with people", bring it to a day: "What would that look like at 10am on a Tuesday?"
- **The why-ladder.** "What did that give you?" … "And what did that give you?" Stop at a quality of work she recognises as hers. Name it in her words; check it ("Is 'being the one who figures it out' right?").
- **Price it.** "What did that cost?" or "When was there too much of it?" Every real preference has a cost.
- **Use her past as a question, never an answer.** "Your thesis had a lot of data analysis in it. Was that the fun part, or the experiments behind it?" Not: "You clearly love data analysis."
- **Permission language.** When she says "I'd let myself…" or "I'm allowed to…", a self-imposed rule may be loosening. Note it as a hunch.
- **"Should" detector.** When a role or industry comes up as "I should probably…", ask: "Whose should is that?" A should is not a pull.
- **Silence is fine.** Don't fill it with options.

## Story pulls

Ask for one; let it be told whole; then ask what it shows.

- "Tell me about a really good day in the lab, or in school, or at a job. What happened?"
- "And a day you went home flat. What made it flat?"
- "When did you last lose track of time doing something?"
- "Tell me about something you did outside your degree that you were proud of."
- "What job have you had, any job, that you liked more than you expected? What about it?"
- "When have people come to you for help? With what?"
- "What did you do when an experiment was running and you had to wait?"
- "Which part of your thesis would you happily do again? Which part would you pay someone else to do?"
- "Who's someone whose job you've envied, even a little? What part?"
- "Picture yourself in two years telling a friend 'work's good'. What's good about it?"

## Kinds of work (probes for each quality)

- **Making vs checking vs explaining.** "Would you rather build the thing, prove the thing works, or explain the thing to someone who needs it?"
- **Bench vs desk vs people.** "On a good week, what share of your time is hands-on, at a screen, and talking with people?"
- **Depth vs variety.** "One hard problem for six months, or six different problems this month?"
- **Certainty vs ambiguity.** "Do you like knowing exactly what done looks like, or working out what done should be?"
- **Pace.** "Did deadlines bring out your best or your worst?"
- **Team.** "Who were you with on your best days? How many people?"
- **Mission.** "Does it matter to you what the work is *for*? Say more."

## Conditions

Ask only when it fits, one at a time: where she can live, in-person or remote, how soon she needs income, a pay floor (only if she raises it), how big a place she'd like, anything she'd turn down regardless.

## After a strong reaction to a card

- Yes: "What about it pulled you?" → tag the quality, not the title.
- No: "Was it the work itself, the setting, or the people you imagined?" A "no" to sales can be a "no" to commission, not to talking with customers.
- Surprise: "That one surprised me. What did you see in it?"

## When she's stuck or low

- "What's one thing on the map you're even slightly curious about?"
- "Who on the map would you most like to hear from?"
- "Forget jobs for a second: what have you enjoyed reading or watching about lately?" (log as an interest hunch)
